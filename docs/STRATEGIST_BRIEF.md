# The Mind Files — Strategist Brief

**Purpose of this file.** It describes everything the mobile studio app currently
does, and every creative lever that can be pulled. A strategist reads it, writes
changes into §8, and the edited file comes back to the engineer, who implements
them.

**How to edit it.** Don't rewrite the descriptive sections — they're a snapshot
of what exists, and changing them silently makes it unclear what's a fact and
what's a request. Put every request in **§8 Change Requests**, using the template
there. Edit the body only to correct something that's factually wrong about the
app today, and say so in §8 if you do.

**Status:** app v1.2.1, Android, single user. Last updated by the engineer.

---

## 1. What the app is

An Android app that runs the whole production pipeline on the phone: write the
script, hand image/video prompts to Google Flow, voice the narration, render the
video with FFmpeg, compose the thumbnail, publish to YouTube, and read back how
published videos performed.

There is no server. Everything lives on the device. It talks to exactly four
services: Gemini, Anthropic, ElevenLabs, YouTube.

**What this means for strategy:** the app can act on anything the YouTube Data
API exposes about the channel's own uploads, and nothing else. It has no access
to YouTube Analytics (watch time, retention curves, traffic sources, impressions,
CTR) — see §5 for exactly where that line falls. That constraint shapes what
advice can actually be automated versus what has to stay a human judgment call.

## 2. The channel as the app currently understands it

This is the brand definition compiled into every generation prompt. It is the
highest-leverage thing on this page: change it and every future script changes.

> **Channel:** "The Mind Files" — investigating why humans think, react,
> sabotage, and love the way they do.
>
> **Tone:** Calm, warm, slightly amused intellectual curiosity. Never preachy,
> never clinical. No medical disclaimers unless the topic is a diagnosable
> pathology.
>
> **Mascot:** Professor Hoot — a distinguished owl in academic tweed waistcoat
> and round spectacles. He reacts to revelations; he never narrates.

**Character rules currently enforced:**
- No occupational costume (scrubs, firefighter gear, pilot jackets) unless the
  script explicitly analyses that profession.
- Wardrobe is relatable and stylish: washed charcoal hoodie, olive chore jacket,
  lilac knit sweater, terracotta corduroy overshirt, similar.
- No archetype repeats within one video, and archetypes from the last 10
  projects are explicitly excluded from new generations.

**Visual identity:** every image/video prompt ends with a fixed "style lock"
string enforcing 2D editorial animation, expressive character acting, cinematic
movement, silent (no audio/voices/music). This is what stops clips looking like
unrelated AI stock footage.

**Categories currently offered in the app:**
Social Dynamics · Attachment & Relationships · Cognitive Bias · Self-Sabotage ·
Emotional Regulation · Persuasion & Influence

## 3. Format specifications

Two formats. Every number here is enforced in code — scripts that break the hard
caps are flagged before they can be voiced.

### Short (9:16)

| Beat | Words | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Hook | 12–16 | Counterintuitive claim that stops the scroll. Generic openers ("Did you know", "Have you ever") are explicitly banned. Open mid-thought, at stakes. |
| Mechanism | 32–38 | One concrete psychological principle, zero academic jargon, grounded in a specific relatable moment. |
| Reframe | 14–18 | Practical takeaway or empowered boundary. Ends on a punchy one-liner. |

- **Total: 55–70 words.** Hard cap 72 — past that the app blocks synthesis.
- At 2.4 words/second this is roughly **23–29 seconds**.
- Three ~10-second continuous video clips, one per beat, with forced character
  continuity between them.

### Long (16:9)

| Section | Words |
| :--- | :--- |
| Cold open | 12–16 |
| Each chapter (3–6 of them) | 70–110 |
| — mini-hook | 12–18 |
| — mechanism | 40–60 |
| — micro-reframe | 16–24 |
| Closing synthesis | 25–35 |

- **Total: 450–700 words**, roughly 3–5 minutes.
- Closing synthesis must end with the catchphrase: *"Stay curious, keep your eyes
  wide, and remember: once you see the pattern, you hold the power."*
- Per chapter: two ~10s hero clips plus one animated still (Ken Burns pan/zoom),
  so the visual changes roughly every 8–12 seconds.

**Cadence law:** all spoken copy is bound to **2.4 words per second**. Word counts
aren't style preferences — they're timing constraints, and exceeding them breaks
the render.

## 4. What happens to a video, end to end

1. **Topic** — typed, or picked from an on-device topic bank the AI can refill.
2. **Script** — generated by Gemini *or* Claude (switchable), validated against
   the word caps and structure above.
3. **Storyboard** — each beat/chapter shows its video prompts with one-tap copy.
   You paste into Google Flow, generate there, import the clip back.
4. **Stills** — the connective still per chapter is generated in-app.
5. **Voice** — ElevenLabs narration with word-level timing. Four voices: Will
   (warm, default), Adam (deep, authoritative), George (British documentary),
   Rachel (clear, neutral).
6. **Assembly** — FFmpeg on the phone: clips speed-matched to narration, captions
   burned in, mascot overlaid.
7. **Thumbnail** — two-line composer: white top line, gold bottom line.
8. **Publish** — straight to YouTube with title, description, tags, privacy.
9. **Library** — reads back what's published, with stats.

**Manual step worth knowing:** video clips are *not* generated in-app. Google
Flow has no public API, so the app's job is to hand over a correct prompt and
take the finished file back. Everything else is automatic.

## 5. What the app can see about performance

This is the boundary that matters most for strategy work. **Be precise about
which column you're asking for** — one is automatable today, the other isn't.

### Available now (YouTube Data API)

Per video: title · description · publish date · thumbnail · duration · **view
count** · **like count** · **comment count** · privacy status.

Per channel: title · subscriber count · total video count · total view count.

### Not available

Watch time · average view duration · **audience retention curves** · impressions
· **click-through rate** · traffic sources · demographics · subscribers gained
per video · swipe-away rate.

Those live in the **YouTube Analytics API**, which is a separate API with a
separate OAuth scope. The app does not currently use it.

**This is worth a decision.** Retention and CTR are the two metrics that actually
explain Shorts performance, and right now the app is optimising against views —
a lagging, noisy proxy. If retention data would change your advice, say so in §8
and the engineer can scope adding it. It is a real piece of work, not a toggle,
but it is possible.

### Shorts detection is a heuristic

The Data API has no "is this a Short" flag. The app classifies anything **≤ 180
seconds** as a Short. A short regular upload gets miscounted. There's an "All"
filter to see everything.

## 6. How performance currently feeds back into writing

When YouTube is connected and the setting is on, the app summarises recent
results into the generation prompt.

**What gets sent:** the channel's median view count, the top 5 performing titles
with their views, and — only when the sample is large enough — the bottom 5.

**What does not get sent:** descriptions, tags, thumbnails, full transcripts.
Deliberately narrow, to avoid teaching the model to imitate its own boilerplate.

**The instruction attached to it:**

> Study what the strongest titles have in common — the shape of the tension they
> promise, how concrete the situation is, whose behaviour is being explained —
> and carry that instinct into this script. Do not copy phrasing, reuse a hook,
> or rewrite an existing topic. This is evidence about what this audience
> responds to, not a template.

**Two guards, both deliberate:**

- **Videos newer than 2 days are excluded.** They haven't had time to accumulate
  views; including them would rank good uploads as failures purely for being
  recent.
- **Below 4 eligible videos, nothing is sent at all.** A confident-sounding
  pattern drawn from two videos is worse than no guidance, because the model will
  follow it.

Both thresholds are tunable — see §8.

## 8. Change Requests

**This is the section to write in.** One numbered block per change. Be concrete —
"punchier hooks" can't be implemented; "hooks must name a specific person or
relationship in the first 6 words" can.

Use this template:

```
### CR-1 — <short title>
**What:** <the change, specifically>
**Where:** <brand bible / short format / long format / categories / performance
context / thumbnail / other>
**Why:** <the reasoning, and any evidence behind it>
**Priority:** <high / medium / low>
```

Worked example, to show the level of detail that's actionable:

```
### CR-0 — Example: tighten the hook
**What:** Cut the hook from 12–16 words to 8–12, and require it to name a
concrete relationship (partner, boss, friend, parent) rather than an abstraction.
**Where:** short format
**Why:** The top performers all name a specific relationship in the first line;
the weakest ones open with an abstract noun like "anxiety" or "the brain".
**Priority:** high
```

Delete CR-0 and write your own below.

## 9. Open questions for the strategist

Answers here shape what gets built next.

1. **Is view count enough?** The app currently optimises against views. Retention
   and CTR would be far better signals, but need the YouTube Analytics API added.
   Worth the work?
2. **Should the format constraints move at all?** The 55–70 word short is tuned
   to ~25 seconds. If the evidence says 40 seconds retains better, the word cap,
   clip count and beat structure all shift together.
3. **How many categories?** Six today. Should they narrow to what performs, or
   widen to test?
4. **Is the catchphrase earning its place?** It costs ~15 words of every long-form
   outro.
5. **What should a "weak" video trigger?** Right now underperformers are shown to
   the model as things to learn from. Should they instead be actively avoided as
   topics, or retried with a different angle?
6. **Thumbnail strategy.** The two-line formula is fixed. Is it right?

## 10. Known limitations — honest list

- **Video clips are manual.** Google Flow has no API. Prompt out, file back in.
- **No retention or CTR data**, as above.
- **Shorts detection is duration-based** and can misclassify.
- **Performance context needs 4+ published videos** older than 2 days to activate.
- **Renders are heavy.** A 4-minute long-form at full resolution is a long encode
  and warms the phone. Draft mode (720p) is the default.
- **Single device.** Projects live on the phone; nothing syncs to the desktop
  studio except by exporting files by hand.
- **ElevenLabs free tier is 10,000 characters/month.** The app hard-blocks at
  9,000 as a safety ceiling and caches every synthesis so identical text is never
  billed twice.
