How every video on The Mind Files gets made

An Android app that takes a psychology topic all the way to a published YouTube video on one phone: script, visuals, voice, edit, thumbnail, upload, and a feedback loop from past results. This brief describes what it does today, the rules it writes by, and the levers a strategist can pull to get more views.

End to end

The production pipeline

Each project moves through these steps in order. Only one step is manual: Google Flow has no public API, so video clips are made there and brought back.

  1. Pick a topic AUTO

    Typed in, or chosen from an on-device Topic Bank that the AI refills eight ideas at a time, weighted toward priority tactics.

  2. Write the script AUTO

    Gemini or Claude writes narration plus a full shot list. Word counts and shot rules are checked before anything is spent. If a model is rate-limited, the app falls back to other models, then to the other provider.

  3. Storyboard the shots MANUAL

    Every shot has a finished prompt with one-tap copy. Clips are generated in Google Flow and imported. Stills are imported (or generated where the key allows). A character reference card keeps the same face across shots.

  4. Voice the narration AUTO

    ElevenLabs reads the script and returns word-level timing, which drives captions and edit timing.

  5. Assemble the video AUTO

    FFmpeg on the phone cuts clips and moving stills to the narration, burns in captions and overlays Hoot. Draft 720p is the default; full 1080p is one switch.

  6. Make the thumbnail AUTO

    A 1280×720 card built from a frame of the video, with a two-line headline and a category badge.

  7. Publish to YouTube AUTO

    Upload with title, description, tags, privacy and thumbnail.

  8. Read results back AUTO

    The Library pulls views, likes and comments for every upload and feeds a summary into the next script.

Formats

These numbers are timing constraints, not style preferences: all narration is paced at 2.4 words per second, so word counts set runtime.

Short (9:16)

BeatWordsJob
Hook12–16A counterintuitive claim that names the tactic. "Did you know" and "Have you ever" openers are banned. Opens mid-thought, at stakes.
Mechanism32–38How the tactic works, no jargon, framed as being done to the viewer in a moment they have lived.
Reframe14–18A takeaway or boundary that hands control back. Ends on a punchy closing line.

A "Three tactics" variant keeps the same word budget and splits the mechanism into three 10–13 word micro-beats, one per named tactic.

The brand bible

How scripts are written

Every generation request starts with the same brand definition. Changing it changes every future script, which makes it the highest-leverage text in the app.

You are the head writer for "The Mind Files", a YouTube channel investigating why humans think, react, sabotage, and love the way they do. Tone: calm, warm, slightly amused intellectual curiosity. Never preachy, never clinical.

Content laws

  • Name the tactic. Every script is about one specific, named mechanism: love bombing, foot-in-the-door, triangulation, sunk cost, anchoring. Generic labels ("manipulation", "the brain") are banned.
  • Point it at the viewer. "This is being done to you" framing, never "how to read someone".
  • No medical disclaimers unless the topic is a diagnosable condition.
  • Hoot never narrates and is never mentioned in the script.

Characters

  • Relatable, stylish wardrobe (charcoal hoodie, olive chore jacket, lilac knit sweater). No occupational costumes unless the topic is that job.
  • Character types from the last 10 projects are excluded, so the cast keeps changing.
  • Each script returns one fixed description of face, hair and wardrobe that is repeated in every shot.

Topic selection

Six categories are offered: Social Dynamics · Attachment & Relationships · Cognitive Bias · Self-Sabotage · Emotional Regulation · Persuasion & Influence. Topic ideas must name a specific tactic and be framed at the viewer. At least half the suggestions centre on five priority tactics picked in an earlier strategy round: love bombing, foot-in-the-door, sunk cost, anchoring bias and triangulation. Gaslighting is deprioritised (falling demand, high competition) but not banned.

Title, description and tags

The script also returns a title (Shorts: "punchy, max 60 characters"; deep dives: max 70), a hook line reused on the thumbnail, and hashtags. Beyond the length cap there are no title rules. A Short's description is the hook line plus the hashtags; a deep dive's description gets chapter timestamps. Everything is editable before upload.

Visuals and shot rules

A beat is a unit of narration; a shot is a unit of picture. Shorts used to come out as one person in one room for 25 seconds, so the shot list is now written and checked against these rules:

RuleValue
Minimum shots per Short4
Longest a shot may hold3 s
Cutaways (character out of frame)≥ 1
Distinct settings2–3
Camera move named in each promptpush in, whip pan, rack focus, overhead, macro insert…
Action must be a verb of change"sitting", "stirring", "staring" and similar are banned
Generated video clips per Short3 by default (one per beat, first on the hook)
Everything elsestills, animated with a slow push-in or pan
  • Why a clip budget: each Flow clip spends paid credits. Stills cost a fraction and still move on screen, so a Short can have six or more shots while paying for three clips.
  • Art style: anime. Every prompt ends with the same style lock, and its last words are always "anime art style".
  • Continuity: every shot that shows the character restates the same face, hair and wardrobe. The Storyboard also offers a character reference image to make first in Flow and attach to every clip.
  • Hoot is never in a prompt. He is added during editing; a generated owl would show up twice.
  • Deep dives: per chapter, two 8–10 s hero clips (the character starts the tension, then escalates it) plus one symbolic still with no people in it.
Packaging

Voice, captions, mascot and thumbnail

Voice

ElevenLabs, multilingual v2 model. Four voices: Will (warm, default), Adam (deep, authoritative), George (British documentary), Rachel (clear, neutral). Word-level timing comes back with the audio.

Captions

One word at a time in Anton, white with a heavy outline, just below the centre of the frame. Words of 7+ letters flash in neon yellow. Matched against a published channel video.

Professor Hoot

Bottom-left, about a fifth of the frame width, on screen for the whole Short. His expression changes per beat: surprised on the hook, thinking on the mechanism, knowing on the reframe (keywords in a line can override this). In deep dives he appears only in the first 3.5 s and last 2.5 s of each chapter.

Thumbnail

1280×720. A blurred, darkened frame fills the card, the same frame sits sharp in a 9:16 panel in the middle, a pill badge reads "PSYCHOLOGY FACT", a two-line headline (yellow, then white) defaults to the hook line, and Hoot appears large at the bottom. The frame is chosen from five sampled from the video.

Distribution

Publishing

  • Uploads go straight to YouTube in category Education, marked not made for kids.
  • Default privacy is private; it can be changed per video or in Settings.
  • Uploads happen immediately. There is no scheduled publish time.
  • The custom thumbnail is set after upload; if YouTube rejects it, the video still goes live.
  • Re-publishing a project asks first, because a second upload creates a duplicate video.
Learning from results

How performance feeds back into writing

When YouTube is connected and the setting is on, each new script request includes a short summary of how past uploads did:

  • The channel's median views and the top 5 titles with their view counts.
  • The bottom 5 titles, labelled "AVOID THESE ANGLES", once there are more than 10 eligible videos.
  • Shorts are compared only with Shorts (anything 3 minutes or under), and only public videos count.
  • Videos newer than 2 days are left out, since they haven't had time to collect views.
  • With fewer than 4 eligible videos nothing is sent, so a pattern from two videos can't steer the writing.
Study what the strongest titles have in common: the shape of the tension they promise, how concrete the situation is, whose behaviour is being explained. Carry that instinct into this script. Do not copy phrasing, reuse a hook, or rewrite an existing topic.

What the app can see

Per video: title, description, publish date, duration, views, likes, comments, privacy. Per channel: subscribers, video count, total views.

What it cannot see

Retention curves, average view duration, swipe-away rate, impressions, click-through rate, traffic sources, demographics. These need the YouTube Analytics API, which is not connected yet.

Who can change what

Levers

In the app, no engineering

  • Script provider and model
  • Clips per Short (Flow budget)
  • Narrator voice
  • Performance feedback on or off
  • Captions, mascot, draft or full quality
  • Default privacy
  • Per video: title, description, tags, thumbnail headline and badge, Short structure (one or three tactics)

Needs a code change

  • Brand bible: tone, laws, character rules
  • Word counts, beat structure, shot rules
  • Categories and priority tactics
  • Title rules
  • Caption style, mascot placement and timing
  • Thumbnail layout
  • Feedback thresholds (2 days, 4 videos, top/bottom 5)
  • Adding YouTube Analytics, music, scheduling
For the strategist

Open opportunities

Things the app does not do today. These are observations, not recommendations; each is buildable if the strategy calls for it.

It optimises for views, not retention or click-through

Retention and CTR explain Shorts performance far better than raw views. Adding the YouTube Analytics API means one extra sign-in permission and a moderate build.

Titles have almost no rules

Only a length cap. There is no title formula, no variant testing, and the feedback loop only learns from titles.

No music or sound design

Videos carry narration only. No background bed, stingers or sound effects.

No posting schedule

Uploads go out when the button is pressed. There is no scheduled publish time or cadence plan.

No loop or call to action

The Short ends on the reframe line. Endings aren't written to loop back into the hook, and there is no follow, comment or series prompt in the video, description or a pinned comment.

Hoot is on screen for the entire Short

Bottom-left, always visible. His role in hook strength and retention hasn't been measured.

Priority tactics are fixed

The five priority tactics were set in an earlier round and don't update from results.

Output volume is capped by voice credits

About 20–25 Shorts a month on the ElevenLabs free tier. Posting more often means a paid voice plan.

How to send changes back

Write each change as its own numbered request. Be concrete: "punchier hooks" can't be built; "hooks must name a specific relationship in the first 6 words" can.

### CR-8 — <short title>
What:     <the change, specifically>
Where:    <brand bible / short format / deep dive / topics /
           titles / captions / mascot / thumbnail / publishing /
           feedback loop / other>
Why:      <reasoning and any evidence>
Priority: <high / medium / low>

Earlier rounds shipped CR-1 to CR-4, CR-6 and CR-7 (name the tactic, point at the viewer, priority tactics, the three-tactics variant, underperformers as an avoid signal, visual dynamism). CR-5, YouTube Analytics, is still open.
